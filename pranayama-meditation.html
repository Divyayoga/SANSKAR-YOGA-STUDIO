Practice responsibly

Your Safety Comes First

Pranayama and meditation should be practiced comfortably and progressively. Breathing practices should never involve unnecessary strain, force or discomfort.

If you have a medical condition, are taking medication, are pregnant, or have been advised to follow specific healthcare guidance, please consult your qualified healthcare professional before beginning or modifying a breathing or meditation practice.

Pranayama and meditation are complementary wellness practices and are not a replacement for medical or mental-health treatment.

Frequently asked questions

Pranayama & Meditation FAQ

Do I need previous experience?

No. Beginners are welcome. We can start with simple breathing awareness and basic meditation techniques and progress gradually.

Can I learn Pranayama online?

Yes. Live online guidance can help you understand breathing technique, posture and practice more clearly. The intensity and techniques should always be appropriate for your experience and comfort.

Can meditation help with stress?

Meditation can be a useful complementary practice for relaxation, mindfulness and stress management. It is not a substitute for professional mental-health treatment when that is needed.

Can Pranayama cure anxiety or depression?

Pranayama and meditation should not be presented as cures for clinical anxiety, depression or other mental-health disorders. They may support relaxation and general wellbeing, while anyone experiencing a mental-health condition should seek appropriate professional care.

How long should I meditate each day?

For beginners, even a short consistent practice can be a useful starting point. Your routine can gradually develop according to your comfort, schedule and experience.

Can international clients join?

Yes. Sessions are designed for online students worldwide. You can practice from your own home or personal space.

How do I book a private session?
